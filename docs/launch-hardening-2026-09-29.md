## Hero comparison vs `hero-recipes.md` (Recipe A, scroll-scrubbed)

"Our hero" here means the scroll-controlled Blender frame sequence. It is the hero on `/cinematic`
(`src/scripts/cinematic.ts`) and the "Inside Study 01" section on `/`, below a still-and-parallax hero
(`src/scripts/sequence.ts`, `src/components/WatchAnatomy.astro`).

Measured: desktop sequence 60 frames, 800×800 WebP with alpha, 2,148 KB total, 35 KB average. Mobile sequence
60 frames, 500×500, 1,184 KB, 19 KB average. Local probe on `/cinematic`: LCP is the `<img>` still
(516 ms desktop, 256–356 ms at 390px, unthrottled). One frame requested before `load`. 66 frame requests and
2.10 MB within 1.5 s after load on desktop; 65 requests and 1.12 MB at 390px.

### What we do better

- **Real 3D source, reproducible.** Frames come from a committed `.blend` and `scripts/render/watch.py`, with
  true occlusion and reflections (`sequence.ts:6-9`). The recipe relies on an AI video generation. Ours has no
  Higgsfield cost, no risk of cuts or flashes, and re-renders deterministically. This is Recipe B's "MODEL"
  discipline applied to Recipe A.
- **Bounded memory.** At most 10 (`sequence.ts:58`) or 14 (`cinematic.ts:81`) decoded `ImageBitmap`s, farthest
  frames `close()`d. The recipe keeps every decoded `Image` alive, 150 of them at 1600px.
- **Fewer bytes for the smoothness.** 60 frames with adjacent-frame cross-blending (`cinematic.ts:62-75`) and
  exponential smoothing (`:128`). 2.1 MB desktop against the recipe's ≤ 6 MB budget.
- **Cache-safe versioning.** A content hash of all frames is appended as `?v=` with `force-cache`
  (`src/data/watch.ts:29-32`), so a re-render can't serve stale frames. The recipe has no versioning.
- **No-JS / reduced motion gets the whole story, not one poster.** All three chapters are real HTML with
  headings, copy and stills. Verified in the no-JS screenshot: complete hero, no blank box. The recipe's fallback
  is a single poster.
- **Accessibility beyond the recipe.** Chapter nav with `aria-current="step"`, off-screen chapters `inert` and
  `aria-hidden`, and focus moved before a panel goes inert (`cinematic.ts:39-53`, `:155-163`). "Skip the film"
  skip link. axe 0 violations across 16 cinematic runs.
- **Live preference changes and edge cases.** It re-initialises when reduced motion toggles
  (`cinematic.ts:183`). A `min-height:600px` guard stops landscape phones from getting a pinned film
  (`cinematic.ts:6`). `saveData` is honoured on both pages. It pauses when the tab is hidden, off-screen
  (`sequence.ts:110-116`, `cinematic.ts:150`), and on bfcache-aware `pagehide`.
- **Failure is verified, not assumed.** A forced 404 on every frame restores the static chapters.

### What we do worse

1. **Frames look soft on HiDPI screens.** The canvas is backed by 800 px (desktop) or 500 px (mobile) bitmaps
   but displayed up to 820 CSS px (`cinematic.css:16`), about 1,640 device px on retina. That's a 2× upscale,
   and it's visibly softer than the 1400 px stills it replaces, so sharpness drops when the scrub takes over.
   The 390px screenshot shows it clearly. Recipe: 1600px frames, canvas sized to `clientWidth × min(DPR, 2)`.
   *Fix:* re-render at 1200–1600px (the budget has room) and size the canvas by DPR.
2. **Frame fetching starts before `load` and isn't progressive.** Frame 0 is fetched before `load`, then
   `warm()` downloads 1 → 59 in order (`cinematic.ts:112-120`) whether or not the visitor scrolls. That's
   2.1 MB desktop and 1.1 MB mobile straight away. The recipe starts after `load` and loads in passes of
   every 16th → 8th → 4th → 2nd → all frames.
3. **No nearest-frame fallback.** `paint()` returns early when the target frame isn't decoded
   (`cinematic.ts:62`; `sequence.ts:47`), so on slow networks a fast scroll freezes on the last frame. The recipe
   draws the nearest loaded frame. This follows from item 2.
4. **Mobile gets a frame sequence.** The recipe gives mobile a poster or a short muted loop. We stream 60 frames
   and scrub a sticky stage. It works, and the QA suites pass at 375/390. But it costs 1.1 MB on phones, and the
   earlier cinematic mobile run recorded 1,438,718 bytes initial transfer (`docs/VELORNE-POLISH-QA.md:49`), just
   under the 1.5 MB first-load budget. Deliberate trade-off, not a bug. Decide whether to keep it.
5. **Two separate scrub engines.** `sequence.ts` (GSAP ScrollTrigger pin, 132 lines) and `cinematic.ts`
   (hand-rolled rAF, 186 lines) duplicate loading, caching and failure logic, and they diverge: only the cinematic
   one blends frames. The recipe has one module behind a `data-scrub-*` API. That's a maintenance cost.
6. **Only 60 source frames.** Blending hides the steps, but a cross-fade of exploded parts can show double
   edges mid-transition, and the recipe's 120–180 frames avoid that. Scrub slowly between chapters on a large
   screen to judge it.
7. **Film-dialog poster loads on every page.** `<video poster="/media/velorne/sequence/000.webp">` inside a
   closed `<dialog>` (`src/components/WatchDialogs.astro`) fetches 31 KB even with JS off or reduced motion. It
   has no `?v=`, so `/cinematic` downloads frame 0 twice. Small fix: set the poster when the dialog opens, or
   reuse the versioned URL.
8. **The main `/` hero isn't the scrub.** On the home page the 3D story sits in section 4. The hero is a still
   with GSAP parallax and tilt (`src/scripts/motion.ts`). This is a design choice, noted because the recipe
   treats the scrub as the hero.

Equal to the recipe: the still `<img>` is the LCP element with `fetchpriority="high"` and explicit dimensions
(`ProductImage.astro:9`). The canvas stays hidden until the first frame decodes. Frames are decoded off-thread
with `createImageBitmap`. Draws happen only when the frame changes.
